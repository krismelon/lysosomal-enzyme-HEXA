## Function

The HEXA gene provides instructions for making the alpha subunit of an enzyme called beta-hexosaminidase A. This enzyme works inside lysosomes, together with GM2 activator protein, to break down GM2 ganglioside, a fatty substance found in cell membranes. This helps prevent GM2 ganglioside from accumulating in nerve cells.

## Review Questions & Answers

**1. What does your enzyme normally break down inside lysosomes?**

Beta-hexosaminidase A helps break down GM2 ganglioside, a fatty substance found in cell membranes. This process helps prevent the substance from building up inside lysosomes.

**2. What is the total length of the protein, and which amino acids are most common?**

The HEXA protein sequence is 529 amino acids long. Based on the amino acid composition graph, leucine (L) is the most common amino acid, with about 59 occurrences, while methionine (M) is the least common, with about 7 occurrences.

**3. How many potential N-glycosylation motifs did you find, and where are they located?**

I found 3 potential N-glycosylation motifs in the HEXA protein sequence. They are located at amino acid positions 115, 157, and 295. The motifs are NYT, NKT, and NNT, respectively.

**4. Why can glycosylation matter for lysosomal proteins? Does finding a motif prove the protein is glycosylated?**

Glycosylation can help lysosomal proteins fold properly, remain stable, and reach the correct location in the cell. However, finding a possible glycosylation motif does not prove that glycosylation actually occurs because the site may not be used by the cell.

**5. What could happen in a cell if the enzyme did not work properly?**

If beta-hexosaminidase A does not work properly, GM2 ganglioside can build up inside lysosomes, especially in nerve cells. This buildup can damage nerve cells and lead to Tay-Sachs disease, which affects the nervous system.
