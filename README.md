## Function

The HEXA gene provides instructions for making the alpha subunit of an enzyme called beta-hexosaminidase A. This enzyme works inside lysosomes, together with GM2 activator protein, to break down GM2 ganglioside, a fatty substance found in cell membranes. This helps prevent GM2 ganglioside from accumulating in nerve cells.

## Review Questions & Answers

**1. What does your enzyme normally break down inside lysosomes?** \

Beta-hexosaminidase A helps break down GM2 ganglioside, a fatty substance found in cell membranes.

**2. What is the total length of your protein, and which amino acids are most common?** 

The HEXA protein has 529 amino acids. Leucine (L) is the most common, followed by serine (S) and valine (V).

**3. How many potential N-glycosylation motifs did you find? Where are they located?** 

I found 3 potential N-glycosylation motifs at amino acid positions 116, 157, and 295.

**4. Why can glycosylation matter for lysosomal proteins? Does finding a motif prove the protein is glycosylated?** 

Glycosylation helps proteins fold properly and reach the right place in the cell. However, finding a motif does not prove that the protein is glycosylated because the pattern only shows a possible site where sugar molecules can attach, not that they actually do.

**5. What could happen in the cell if your enzyme did not work properly?** 

If the enzyme does not work properly, GM2 ganglioside can build up inside lysosomes and damage nerve cells. This can lead to Tay-Sachs disease.
